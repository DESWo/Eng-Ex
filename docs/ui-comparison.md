# UI comparison report

Two-way audit, August 8 2026: Engineering Explorer against (1) the recognizable
hallmarks of generic AI-built websites and (2) the craft bar of professionally
built products (Brilliant, Duolingo, Khan Academy, Linear, Stripe). Every claim
carries code evidence; both directions are reported honestly.

## Part 1 — Against generic AI-built sites

**Distinctiveness score: 7.5/10** (0 = indistinguishable from a template, 10 = fully distinctive)

Audit of /Users/desmond/Documents/GitHub/engineering-explorer (landing components, pages, index.css, index.html, disciplines.ts/about.ts copy, plus repo-wide greps). The site decisively avoids the loudest AI-site tells: no purple-blue-on-dark gradients, no gradient text, no glassmorphism cards, no Inter, no emoji-as-icons, no hype copy or buzzwords (zero hits for unleash/elevate/seamless/etc.), no fake testimonials, no Get Started CTAs, and zero stock/raster imagery - every visual is seeded rough.js sketch SVG, bespoke DIY diagrams, or pure-CSS graph paper. The identity layer (cream paper #faf7f2, Source Serif 4 + IBM Plex system with mono tabular numerals on data, color-mix accent derivation, hand-drawn card frames, state-aware CTAs, physically honest copy) is coherent, opinionated, and annotated with design rationale in comments, which reads as authored, not generated. What keeps it off a 9: a handful of 2025-template reflexes survive at the layout/motion layer - most visibly three animated blur-3xl floating blobs behind the hero (Hero.tsx:28-45, the era's most recognizable treatment, albeit at 15% opacity in brand colors), the pill badge above the H1, centered section headers with eyebrow chips over icon-card grids, uniform heavy border radii, whileInView fade-up staggers on every section, and a faint lucide-Sparkles motif. These are skeleton-level habits under a genuinely distinctive skin: at a glance the site reads as an engineer's notebook, not a template; on close inspection of the hero you can still see the year it was built in. Score 7.5/10 distinctive. Quickest wins if the owner wants to push toward 9: replace the hero blobs with something from the notebook language (e.g. rough.js marginalia or drafting doodles), drop or restyle the hero badge pill, and vary the section-entrance motion.

## Part 2 — Against professionally built sites

Engineering Explorer sits well above hobby grade and within reach of the Brilliant/Duolingo tier: it has a real design language (the academic-notebook concept is executed, not just claimed), real primitives, a professionally consistent voice, designed loading/empty/error/locked states, and accessibility work on its custom game interactions that most funded products never do. What separates it from professional-grade is concentrated and fixable rather than structural. One high-severity issue: white-on-accent text on the app's core buttons, chips, and steppers fails WCAG AA contrast for roughly half the twelve discipline accents (the yellow accent lands near 1.9:1 against a 4.5:1 requirement); the fix is a per-accent readable-foreground decision (dark text on light accents, as Duolingo does on yellow) plus a focus trap, scroll lock, and skip link, all afternoon-scale work. Two medium-severity systemic gaps: the color system's missing semantic layer (dark mode via ~1,160 inline overrides, one text pair repeated 147 times, discipline accents duplicated by hand in Hero and Confetti where they will go stale on retheme) and component drift in embryo (a hand-rolled button in the app error fallback, the selected/done/idle chip styling copy-pasted across four components instead of one Chip primitive). Motion is systematized and reduced-motion-safe but a notch denser than pro restraint, mainly because flow steps replay their full entrance choreography on every revisit. Everything else, spacing, type, density, onboarding, responsive behavior, is at or near standard with only low-severity polish items (a resume affordance for returning students, 44px touch targets on the level rail, self-hosted fonts).

### Where it meets or beats professional practice

- The accent theming mechanism is better engineered than most professional token pipelines at this scale: one hex per discipline in data (disciplines.ts) drives coordinated light and dark tints, borders, and grid backgrounds through color-mix in oklab (index.css:66-93, 140-224), so adding discipline #13 needs zero new CSS.
- Mono-for-measurements discipline: every number that measures something is IBM Plex Mono with tabular-nums, app-wide, by convention. Columns of live values never jitter. This is textbook technical typography most edu products skip.
- Microcopy voice is professionally consistent and honest: errors state cause and action, the no-password dialog explains the actual security model to students, star ratings justify themselves in one line, and the 404 stays in character.
- Reduced-motion coverage across all three animation systems (Framer MotionConfig, CSS keyframes media query, the Ticker hook), not just the easy one.
- Accessibility on the custom interactions is rare-tier: role=slider with aria-valuetext and arrow-key paths on direct-manipulation SVG drag handles in 9+ games, plus aria-live result regions with reserved height in ~20 games. Most professional products never retrofit game canvases this way.
- Error containment is deploy-aware: per-game keyed ErrorBoundaries so one stale chunk breaks one game, with copy that correctly names the deploy-replaced-hashed-files cause and a safe recovery.
- The rough.js sketch system shows real craft: stable geometry-derived seeds so shapes never crawl between renders, quantized ResizeObserver redraws, and CSS-class-driven stroke colors (ui/Sketchy.tsx:16-24, SketchFrame.tsx:30-39). All hand-tuned generative drawing, fully compliant with the no-stock-assets rule.
- Design rationale lives next to the code as comments (Reveal's restraint argument, Objective's once-only sheen, ConceptCard's earned-hint history), which is what real design systems do and hobby projects never do.
- FOUC-free theme boot: the saved theme applies in a head script before first paint, with a documented decision to default light rather than follow OS dark (index.html:32-41).
- Perceived-performance details: games code-split per chunk with hover/focus preloading on the picker chips (ChallengeStep.tsx:69-70).
